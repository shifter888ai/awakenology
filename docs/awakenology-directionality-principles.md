# Awakenology: Directionality of Hypnosis and Awakenosis

**Status:** Working conceptual note — record of the discussion on 31 October 2026. This is not yet a revision of the course formulas.

## Core principle

Within the Awakenology framework, the key distinction between **hypnosis (催眠)** and **awakenosis (催醒)** is **directionality**.

The same consciousness, behavior, or concept may be the subject of either process:

- **Hypnosis:** subjective magnification or minimization.
- **Awakenosis:** objective observation, rather than subjective magnification or minimization.

These are the user's framework-specific definitions. They should not be silently replaced with general psychological or scientific definitions.

## Reference point and opposing directions

- **a₀:** the original state / origin point.
- **a₋₁ and a₊₁:** two directions opposite to one another relative to a₀.

Do not assign hypnosis or awakenosis exclusively to a₋₁ or a₊₁ until the user explicitly defines that mapping. Do not infer that either sign automatically means increase or decrease.

## Keep the three directional terms distinct

The terms **递增**, **递减**, and **递归** describe different directions or processes in this framework; they must not be treated as interchangeable variants of one operation.

- **递增:** increasing. The exact quantity/state that increases, and its starting point and destination, must be stated in context.
- **递减:** decreasing. The exact quantity/state that decreases, and its starting point and destination, must be stated in context.
- **递归:** in this Awakenology framework, a layer-by-layer process of passing through layers and returning toward the original point a₀. The return is essential to this framework-specific use of the term.

The user's character-based explanation is:
- **递:** one layer at a time / passing along successively.
- **归:** return.

This is the user's conceptual definition in Awakenology. It is not a claim that the standard computer-science term *recursion* necessarily means returning to an original state. In computer science, recursion refers to a process/function referring to or invoking itself and does not, by itself, guarantee a return to an origin.

## Course-writing rules

1. Describe directionality explicitly; do not describe a process only by saying that consciousness becomes “larger” or “smaller.”
2. For each process, specify its starting point, direction of movement, and destination.
3. Keep subjective magnification/minimization distinct from objective observation.
4. Do not assume that objective observation and return to a₀ are identical; their relationship needs an explicit definition.
5. Do not map a₋₁ and a₊₁ to hypnosis, awakenosis, increase, or decrease without the user's explicit definition.
6. Do not silently change existing formulas, examples, or course wording while this terminology is being clarified.

## Items still to define

Before revising course formulas, establish:
- What exactly increases in 递增 and what exactly decreases in 递减.
- The starting point and destination for each of these two processes.
- How a₋₁ and a₊₁ relate to hypnosis and awakenosis, if either has a specific mapping.
- How objective observation relates to the layer-by-layer return to a₀.

## Short summary

**Hypnosis:** subjective magnification or minimization.  
**Awakenosis:** objective observation.  
**a₀:** origin.  
**a₋₁ / a₊₁:** opposite directions relative to the origin.  
**递增 / 递减 / 递归:** three distinct directional concepts; define each by its direction, starting point, and destination.
